## Partner models

Cloud vendors often organize partnerships around recurring infrastructure revenue and reseller margins:

- **Wholesale or reseller:** The partner purchases capacity at a discount, bills the customer, and takes on credit risk and billing support. This can make dedicated provider support more accessible.
- **Referral or agency:** The customer opens an account and pays the provider directly. This supports customer ownership, but provider support is often self-service until referrals generate significant aggregate spend.

The proposed offering follows the referral or agency model. Provider programs, eligibility, billing arrangements, and benefits need to be confirmed with each vendor.

## Economics at early scale

The source strategy estimates infrastructure at roughly €50–€120 per customer per month. Ten customers would therefore represent around €500–€1,200 in monthly cloud spending, while a 5,000 DKK monthly service price would yield 50,000 DKK in monthly service revenue. These figures are illustrative, exclude costs and taxes, and are not a forecast.

The relatively small infrastructure spend may not justify a dedicated account manager or co-selling funds from a provider. The customer's direct payment also means MoM does not earn an infrastructure margin.

## Potential partners

- **Cloud providers:** Explore service or consulting partner programs with Scaleway and OVHcloud, including their directories, technical support, and customer-owned billing options. Confirm whether each provider supports the desired account-management arrangement.
- **Advisory firms:** GDPR consultants, NIS2 auditors, and digital sovereignty advisors in Denmark and the Nordics may need an implementation partner to operate the stack after their advisory work.
- **Open-source agencies:** European Nextcloud, GitLab, ISO 27001, and GDPR consultancies may be complementary partners for customers moving away from existing hosted development tools.
